GOAL

You are a Saint Bernard guarding a flock on a Swiss alp ("Weide" means pasture). You start with one ram (male, grey, curled horns) and one ewe (female, white). Keep them fed and safe, so they have lambs, and grow the largest flock you can.

The game is over when all rams or all ewes are gone, because the flock can't grow anymore. The game-over screen shows the day you reached, your largest flock, lambs born, sheep killed by wolves, sheep that starved or froze, and the wolf setting you played on.

DAY, NIGHT & SEASONS

A DAY

  • The icon next to the clock shows the sun, a rising or setting sun (morning / evening), or the moon.
  • At 5:30 the shepherd lets the flock out in front of the stable. The day ends at midnight.
  • Day: graze, explore, chase off day wolves.
  • Evening: it gets dark; bring every sheep into the stable. Arrows at the door blink while sheep are still outside.
  • Night: at nightfall the wolf pack arrives. If every sheep is inside, the night passes 6× faster.
  • Midnight: hunger and health are settled, lambs are born, grass has regrown somewhere else. The dog's stamina and food bowl are refilled.
  • Clock speed: about 5.5 s per hour during the day and 7 s per hour at night.

SHEEP

MOVING

  • Sheep walk away from the dog when it is within 130 px. The closer the dog, the faster they go (top speed 100, slower than a wolf). That's how you herd.
  • The stable wall blocks the dog: a sheep inside ignores a dog outside, and the other way round.
  • On their own, sheep roam, pause, sometimes trot off, and loosely keep together.
  • A calm sheep within 50 px of grass walks onto it and stays there.
  • During the day, sheep in the stable walk back out by themselves.
  • In the evening and at night, sheep within 110 px of the door walk in by themselves.
  • When a wolf is close they panic and scatter in random directions.

FOOD & HEALTH

  • Sheep eat only on grass, only during day and evening, and only while the dog keeps its distance. They eat 5% per second.
  • The ring around a sheep shows today's food: yellow while eating, green = fully fed (100%). Food resets every morning.
  • At midnight: a sheep that is not fully fed loses 10–30% health; a fully fed sheep gains 5–25%.
  • Sheep outside the stable at night get cold: about 5% health over a whole night (much more in winter).
  • A wolf bite costs 45% health per second of contact. Bitten sheep can survive, but they need to be well fed.
  • A sheep dies when its health reaches 0. The health bar under a sheep shows up once it has lost health.

LAMBS

  • At midnight every pair of a fully fed ram and a fully fed ewe gets one lamb. For example 2 rams + 2 ewes = 2 lambs, 3 rams + 1 ewe = 1 lamb.
  • Lambs are randomly male or female.

THE DOG

  • Top speed 190. Running costs stamina (6% per second at full speed, 15% more in summer). Standing still restores 6% per second.
  • Below 30% stamina the dog runs at 70% speed (and pants); below 10% only at 45%, slower than sheep and wolves.
  • The food bowl right of the stable restores stamina 3× faster (18% per second). The bowl holds 100%, and it empties by the same amount the dog gains. It is refilled every morning.
  • A wolf may bite the dog: −15% stamina per bite.
  • When bored (standing still 4–8 s) the dog chases its tail or rolls on its back. Click or press a key to snap it out.

WOLVES

HOW MANY

  • Night pack = (sheep ÷ 2, rounded down, + 1) × (1 + wolf setting), then randomly ±20%, at least 1. They arrive a few seconds apart.
  • Day attacks start on day 2: 35% chance on day 2, +10% per day, up to 90%. A day pack is about half the night pack.

BEHAVIOUR

  • Wolves go for the nearest sheep outside the stable. They can never enter the stable.
  • They keep away from the dog (within 150 px) but try to slip around it.
  • Day wolves lose courage while being chased and give up after a few seconds of pressure. Night wolves never give up; they leave at midnight.
  • After killing a sheep a wolf stops to eat for 2.5 s.
  • A wolf within 100 px of the dog may turn on it (about 30% chance per second, ×1.5 at night): it growls, charges, bites, backs off and waits 6–12 s before trying again.
  • Wolves within 120 px of the dog's bowl go and eat the dog food (as fast as the dog would) while it lasts. You can use that to keep them busy.
  • Sheep hiding under a tree stay still and are only spotted by a wolf within 55 px (not in winter).

THE PASTURE

  • Grass patches: 3–6 (3 + 1 per 4 sheep). Total food ≈ sheep × 100% × 1.3, so everyone can get full if you move the flock between patches. In winter it's 30% less. Every night the grass regrows somewhere else.
  • Trees (3–5), rocks (3–6) and lakes (1–3) are placed randomly every new game. Trunks, rocks and lakes block everyone. The dog and wolves steer around them; sheep just slide along.
  • The stable has a roof. It turns see-through while sheep (or the dog) are inside. Two lamps next to the door switch on when it gets dark.
  • Ducks and bees (spring and summer, around the trees) are just for fun. In fall the wind blows the fallen leaves around.

OPTIONS

Wolves: set the number of wolves from −50% to +50% in 10% steps (EASY ≤ −30%, NORMAL −20…+10%, HARD +20…+30%, BRUTAL +40…+50%).

Start season: start the game in spring, summer, fall or winter. The seasons then follow in the usual order, 3 days each. Starting in winter is the hardest.

Shuffle map: the map behind the start screen (trees, rocks, lakes, grass) is the map you will play on. SHUFFLE MAP makes a new random one; it stays the same until you shuffle again.

Your browser remembers the wolf and season settings.
