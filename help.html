GOAL

You are a Saint Bernard guarding a flock on a Swiss alp ("Weide" means pasture). You start with one ram (male, grey, curled horns) and one ewe (female, white). Keep them fed and safe, so they have lambs, and grow the largest flock you can.

The game is over when all rams or all ewes are gone, because the flock can't grow anymore. The game-over screen shows the day you reached, your largest flock, lambs born, sheep killed by wolves, sheep that starved or froze, and the wolf setting you played on.

DAY, NIGHT & SEASONS

A DAY

  • The icon next to the clock shows the sun, a rising or setting sun (morning / evening), or the moon.
  • At {{hour:CLOCK.start}} the shepherd lets the flock out in front of the stable (sheep that spent the night outside are still where they were). The day ends at {{hour:CLOCK.end}}.
  • Day: graze, explore, chase off day wolves.
  • Evening: it gets dark; bring every sheep into the stable. Arrows at the door blink while sheep are still outside.
  • Night: at nightfall the wolf pack arrives. If every sheep is inside, the night passes 6× faster.
  • Midnight: hunger and health are settled, lambs are born, grass has regrown somewhere else. The dog's stamina and food bowl are refilled.
  • Clock speed: about {{CLOCK.daySecPerHour}} s per hour during the day and {{CLOCK.nightSecPerHour}} s per hour at night.

SHEEP

MOVING

  • Sheep walk away from the dog when it is within {{CFG.sheep.dogRadius}} px. The closer the dog, the faster they go (top speed {{CFG.sheep.maxSpeed}}, slower than a wolf). That's how you herd.
  • The stable wall blocks the dog: a sheep inside ignores a dog outside, and the other way round.
  • On their own, sheep roam, pause, sometimes trot off, and loosely keep together.
  • During the day, a hungry calm sheep slowly walks towards grass it can find within {{CFG.sheep.grassSeek}} px (taking breaks on the way). It doesn't wade through a stream for it; frozen streams in winter are fine.
  • A calm sheep within {{CFG.sheep.grassAttract}} px of grass walks onto it and stays there.
  • The pull of a patch scales with how much grass is left: a full patch draws sheep from the whole distance, a half-eaten one only from half as far, and so on. Sheep prefer fuller patches. An eaten-up patch (less than 6% left) draws no one any more.
  • During the day, sheep in the stable walk back out by themselves.
  • In the evening and at night, every fully fed sheep walks back to the stable by itself at its normal pace, from anywhere on the pasture (over a bridge if it is across a stream). Hungry sheep keep eating or stroll around: the dog has to bring them in. Once a hungry sheep stands on the cobblestone patio in front of the door, it walks in by itself.
  • Parking: during the day, a fully fed sheep within {{CFG.sheep.parkRadius}} px of the stable stays there and waits (it doesn't go in). In the evening it walks in by itself.
  • When a wolf is close they panic and scatter in random directions.

FOOD & HEALTH

  • Sheep eat only on grass, only during day and evening, and only while the dog keeps its distance. They eat {{CFG.sheep.eatRate}}% per second.
  • The ring around a sheep shows today's food: yellow while eating, green = fully fed (100%). Food resets every morning.
  • At midnight: a sheep that is not fully fed loses {{CFG.sheep.hungerMin}}–{{CFG.sheep.hungerMax}}% health; a fully fed sheep gains {{CFG.sheep.recoverMin}}–{{CFG.sheep.recoverMax}}%.
  • Sheep outside the stable at night get cold: about {{CFG.sheep.coldNight}}% health over a whole night (much more in winter).
  • A wolf bite costs {{CFG.wolf.biteRate}}% health per second of contact. Bitten sheep can survive, but they need to be well fed.
  • A sheep dies when its health reaches 0. The health bar under a sheep shows up once it has lost health.

LAMBS

  • At midnight every pair of a fully fed ram and a fully fed ewe in the stable gets one lamb. Sheep that spend the night outside don't count. For example 2 rams + 2 ewes = 2 lambs, 3 rams + 1 ewe = 1 lamb.
  • Lambs are randomly male or female.
  • A newborn lamb is {{pct:CFG.sheep.lambSize}}% of the size of a grown sheep and needs only {{pct:CFG.sheep.lambFood}}% of the food to be full (so it fills up faster and eats less grass). The next morning it is fully grown.
  • Growing up: born → small for 1 day → grown up for 1 day → can have lambs from its night {{CFG.sheep.matureNights}} on (fed and in the stable, like everyone else).

THE DOG

  • Top speed {{CFG.dog.speed}}. Running costs stamina ({{CFG.dog.staminaDrain}}% per second at full speed, {{more:CFG.summer.dogDrain}}% more in summer). Standing still restores {{CFG.dog.staminaRecover}}% per second.
  • Below {{CFG.dog.tiredAt}}% stamina the dog runs at {{pct:CFG.dog.tiredSpeed}}% speed (and pants); below {{CFG.dog.exhaustedAt}}% only at {{pct:CFG.dog.exhaustedSpeed}}%, and with no stamina left at all only at {{pct:CFG.dog.emptySpeed}}%, slower than sheep and wolves.
  • The dog is called {{CFG.dog.name}} (you can rename it below, under "Tuning the game"). It has its own hut with its name on it (left, below the flag). Send it in and let it rest: it falls asleep (Zzzzzz), its nose peeking out of the door, and gets its stamina back twice as fast. Move it out again to wake it up.
  • The food bowl right of the stable restores stamina {{CFG.bowl.rate}}× faster ({{bowlrate}}% per second). The bowl holds {{CFG.bowl.food}}%, and it empties by the same amount the dog gains. It is refilled every morning.
  • A wolf may bite the dog: −{{CFG.wolf.dogBite}}% stamina per bite.
  • Barking (W or the WOOF! button): for {{CFG.dog.barkTime}} s the dog reaches further. Sheep move away from up to {{CFG.dog.barkSheep}}× the usual distance, and wolves keep away from up to {{CFG.dog.barkWolf}}× their usual distance and don't attack the dog (an attack under way is broken off). Every sheep out on the pasture answers with a "Bahh!", which helps to find them in the fog. After a bark the dog needs {{CFG.dog.barkCooldown}} s before it can bark again.
  • When bored (standing still 4–8 s) the dog chases its tail or rolls on its back. Click or press a key to snap it out.

WOLVES

HOW MANY

  • Night pack = {{CFG.pack.perSheep}} × sheep{{CFG.pack.power}} × (1 + wolf setting), then randomly ±{{pct:CFG.pack.randomness}}%, at least 1. With power 0.5 this is the square root: a bigger flock brings more wolves, but fewer wolves per sheep. On the normal setting (before the random part): 2 sheep → {{wolves:2}}, 4 → {{wolves:4}}, 9 → {{wolves:9}}, 16 → {{wolves:16}}, 25 → {{wolves:25}}, 36 → {{wolves:36}} wolves. They arrive a few seconds apart.
  • Day attacks start on day 2: every day there is a {{pct:CFG.pack.dayChance}}% chance that a pack comes during the day. A day pack is about {{pct:CFG.pack.dayFraction}}% of the night pack.

BEHAVIOUR

  • Wolves go for the nearest sheep outside the stable. They can never enter the stable.
  • They keep away from the dog (within {{CFG.wolf.fearRadius}} px) but try to slip around it.
  • Day wolves lose courage while being chased and give up after a few seconds of pressure. Night wolves never give up; they leave at midnight.
  • After killing a sheep a wolf stops to eat for {{CFG.wolf.eatTime}} s.
  • A wolf within {{CFG.wolf.attackRange}} px of the dog may turn on it (about {{pct:CFG.wolf.attackChance}}% chance per second, ×1.5 at night): it growls, charges, bites, backs off and waits 6–12 s before trying again.
  • Wolves within {{CFG.bowl.wolfSmell}} px of the dog's bowl go and eat the dog food (as fast as the dog would) while it lasts. You can use that to keep them busy.
  • Sheep hiding under a tree stay still and are only spotted by a wolf within {{CFG.obstacles.wolfSeesHidden}} px (not in winter).

THE PASTURE

  • Grass patches: {{CFG.grass.minPatches}}–{{CFG.grass.maxPatches}} ({{CFG.grass.minPatches}} + 1 per 4 sheep). Total food ≈ sheep × 100% × {{CFG.grass.surplus}}, so everyone can get full if you move the flock between patches. In winter it's {{less:CFG.grass.winterFactor}}% less. Every night the grass regrows somewhere else.
  • Streams ({{range:CFG.streams.count}}) meander across corners of the pasture (never the stable's corner). Together they cut off {{pct:CFG.streams.cutOff.0}}–{{pct:CFG.streams.cutOff.1}}% of the pasture, which you can only reach by crossing water. Long streams have two bridges. Wading through is slow: sheep move at {{pct:CFG.streams.sheepSpeed}}% speed, the dog and wolves at {{pct:CFG.streams.dogSpeed}}%. Each stream has a wooden bridge: herd the sheep across it to keep them moving. Calm sheep don't wade in by themselves. Sometimes a grass patch grows on the far side of a stream. In winter the streams freeze, and everyone can walk straight across.
  • Trees ({{range:CFG.obstacles.trees}}), rocks ({{range:CFG.obstacles.rocks}}) and lakes ({{range:CFG.obstacles.lakes}}) are placed randomly every new game. Trunks, rocks and lakes block everyone. The dog and wolves steer around them; sheep just slide along.
  • The stable has a roof. It turns see-through while sheep (or the dog) are inside. Two lamps next to the door switch on when it gets dark, and on snowy winter days they stay on all day.
  • Ducks and bees (spring and summer, around the trees) are just for fun. In fall the wind blows the fallen leaves around.
  • The farmer's tractor: on {{CFG.tractor.daysPerSeason}} day(s) per season the gate in the top fence opens at {{hour:CFG.tractor.arrive}}, and the tractor drives in and parks on its tracks next to the stable. It backs out {{CFG.tractor.leaveEarly}} hours before evening and the gate closes again.
    • It is solid: nobody can walk through it.
    • It honks at animals in its way and waits until they move. Sheep within {{CFG.tractor.scareRadius}} px of a moving tractor step aside.
    • Wolves keep {{CFG.tractor.wolfFear}} px away from it, so the spot next to the tractor (and the dog's bowl) is safe that day.
    • The gate is open, but the sheep can't leave the pasture.
    • The farmer gets off and walks over the patio into the stable. After a while he comes out for the job of the day, and about {{CFG.farmer.walkBack}} hour before the tractor leaves he walks back and drives off. He has two jobs per season, each on its own random day (so with {{CLOCK.seasonDays}}-day seasons, one day has no tractor):
      • Spring: a beer on the bench next to the patio, or he digs five holes below the patio and plants flowers. They sprout, bud and bloom over the next days, wilt in summer, dry up in fall and are gone in winter.
      • Summer: a beer on the bench, or he mows the lawn in stripes on a piece of meadow near the patio (never on the sheep's grass patches). The stripes fade as the grass grows back.
      • Fall: he rakes the leaves off the patio into heaps (more leaves land there every night), or he goes apple picking with his wheelbarrow at the 1–3 nearest trees (within {{CFG.farmer.appleRadius}} px of the stable, without wading through a stream) and wheels the apples into the stable. With no apple tree close enough he rakes instead.
      • Winter: he shovels the patio almost clear of snow (it's snowed over again the next morning), or he builds a snowman next to the patio: three rolled snowballs, coal eyes, a carrot nose, stick arms and a red scarf. It stays all winter and melts on the first day of spring. You'll see his boot prints and the snowball tracks in the snow.
      Sheep within {{CFG.farmer.sheepRadius}} px make way for him, and wolves keep {{CFG.farmer.wolfFear}} px away. When the dog comes within {{CFG.farmer.petRadius}} px, it wags its tail and gets a pat.

OPTIONS

Wolves: set the number of wolves from −50% to +50% in 10% steps (EASY ≤ −30%, NORMAL −20…+10%, HARD +20…+30%, BRUTAL +40…+50%).

Start season: start the game in spring, summer, fall or winter. The seasons then follow in the usual order, 3 days each. Starting in winter is the hardest.

Shuffle map: the map behind the start screen (trees, rocks, lakes, grass) is the map you will play on. SHUFFLE MAP makes a new random one; it stays the same until you shuffle again.

Relax mode (button left of START GAME, or the R key): no wolves at all, neither by day nor at night. Just herding for fun. Everything else stays the same: the sheep still need grass, lambs are still born, and sheep left outside at night still get cold.

Sound (megaphone button or the M key): the game is full of sounds. The dog's bark, the sheep, the wolves' howls, the ducks, the crickets, the farmer's lawn mower, his beer bottle and the streams are recordings (the files in the soundeffects folder); everything else is made in your browser. Your dog is the listener: the closer a sound is to the dog, the louder it is, and it comes from the left or the right (with headphones or stereo speakers). A sound {{CFG.sound.halfDist}} px from the dog is half as loud; wolves' howls carry 3× as far. Very handy in the fog: bark, and the flock answers with a "Bäh!" from wherever they are.

  • Dog: barks (WOOF!), woofs at the sheep, yelps when bitten, yawns when it wakes up, snores in its hut and pants when it is tired.
  • Sheep: bleat now and then, panic when a wolf is near, shiver in the cold. Every sheep has its own voice: rams are lower, lambs high. Sheep inside the stable sound muffled.
  • Wolves: howl (louder at night, with an echo over the alp) and growl when they go for the dog.
  • Ducks: a quiet quack now and then, the chatter of the whole flock when the dog comes close to a lake, and loud quacking when the dog scares them.
  • Birds (you hear them in the trees): every tree has its own kind of songbird. A dawn chorus in the morning, lots of song in spring, less in summer and fall, and none in winter (no owl either). They go quiet when wolves are about and in a blizzard. In spring a cuckoo calls, and at night an owl hoots.
  • Crickets: chirp softly on summer evenings and through the night (they fade in during the first hour of the evening).
  • Bees: buzz around the trees in spring and summer (by day, close to the tree only).
  • Tractor: its diesel engine chugs while it drives, it honks, and it beeps when it backs out.
  • Farmer: his lawn mower runs while he mows (summer), and you hear the beer bottle open when he sits down on the bench.
  • Streams: babble quietly when the dog is close to one (not in winter: they are frozen).
  • Wind: a breeze that swells with the gusts: calm in summer and on foggy mornings, stronger in fall and winter, much louder while it snows (the heavier the snowfall, the louder) and a howling gale in a blizzard.

Your own recordings: put sound files into the soundeffects folder, named dog_bark_1.mp3, sheep_1.mp3, sheep_2.mp3, wolf_1.mp3, duck_1.mp3, duck_ambient.mp3, crickets.mp3, grassmower.mp3, beeropen.mp3, stream.mp3 … (a random one of each kind plays each time), then run python3 soundeffects/make_sounds_js.py. It packs them into soundeffects/sounds.js, which the game loads (that also works when you open the game straight from your disk). Silence at the start and end is cut off, and all recordings are made equally loud. Sounds that play over and over (lawn mower, stream) are short clips made to loop without a seam; the full-length originals are kept in soundeffects/originals. Only sounds.js is needed to play, so git ignores the sound files themselves.

Browsers only allow sound after your first click or key press. On an iPhone/iPad, check that the ringer switch / volume is up.

Saving: when you leave a game with PAUSE → EXIT GAME (or close the browser tab), the game is saved in your browser. The game is also saved every morning. The start screen then shows RESUME GAME (or press ENTER) next to NEW GAME (or press N). A resumed game keeps its own map, wolf, relax and season settings; the options on the start screen are for new games. There is one saved game: exiting a new game replaces it, and when a game is over its save is deleted.

Your browser remembers the wolf and season settings. Relax mode is always off when you open the game.

TUNING THE GAME

All of these come from config.js (next to game.js). You can change them right here: type a new value and press Enter (or click elsewhere). Lists like 0.85, 0.9 are written with commas. Distances are in pixels (the pasture is 960 × 640), times in seconds.

  • If you opened this page from the game, the game uses the new values right away.
  • Your changes are remembered by this browser, so they still apply when you start the game later. The start screen then shows "(custom settings)" next to "detailed gameplay".
  • A web page can't change files on your disk. To make your settings permanent (or put them in git), click Download config.js and replace the old config.js in the Weide folder with it.
